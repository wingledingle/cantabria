Scheduled

Lead azide detonation at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant

The Army plans two controlled blasts of about 9,700 lb of degraded lead azide (68 drums, about 6,900 lb of lead) inside earth-covered magazines near Middletown, Iowa. This page follows the timing, the blast-hour wind, and where the open Iowa Lead Tracker model says the lead could settle.

EPA permit weather check, hour by hour

The emergency permit bars the blast with surface wind under 1 or over 15 mph, wind over 30 mph at 1,000–3,000 ft, or rain. Each cell: surface wind, direction it blows from, and the strongest wind at 1,000–3,000 ft.

The permit also bars thunderstorms, fog, a low overcast and an inversion, which a point forecast can't settle. All four models show 100% cloud cover this morning. Wind speeds are model forecasts at the plant, not measurements.

Official guidance

What officials are telling people

Relocation zone

FEMA is supporting relocation of all residents of Middletown and the immediate area, starting Nov. 24. The zone is a 5-mile cone southwest of the plant, one mile past the Army's modeled path.

Everyone else nearby

The Army asks people outside the zone to shelter in place with windows and doors closed. Inside the 5 µg/ft² line, stay indoors; inside 100, also wet-clean surfaces children touch.
